### Gotchas

- The rulebook's own baseline design (Mochiswarm/BlimpSwarm) has NO documented buoyancy-trim or thrust-verification procedure anywhere — teams following the reference build faithfully will still show up untested on these two hard numeric rules (±10gf, 100gf) unless they build their own bench test.
- Sim buoyancy uses an artificial 'gravcomp' multiplier on top of fluid physics — do not copy the sim's mass/balloon-size numbers onto the real vehicle's design budget; they were tuned to make the sim behave, not derived from a real mass/lift calculation.
- Sim air density is hardcoded to sea-level (1.225 kg/m^3); Bogotá is ~2,600m altitude (~0.90 kg/m^3 air, and correspondingly less helium lift per liter) — edit the MuJoCo model's density before trusting sim margins, and expect real lift/thrust response to be measurably worse than what the sim shows.
- The sim's default waypoint TARGET_SEQUENCE is a 9-color rainbow list that does not match the actual 2026 rulebook course (4 identical red balloons, order given per run) — this part of the sim cannot be used as-is for pre-flight logic validation until re-parameterized.
- Vision (color-blob tracking) runs on a SEPARATE MCU (Arduino Nicla Vision + custom PCB) in the reference design, not on the ESP32-S3 itself — confirm whether the professor's PCB includes/expects a companion vision board, since this changes the bring-up plan substantially.
- Color thresholds for balloon detection are lighting-condition-specific and manually tuned (per BlimpSwarm's own docs) — budget an on-site re-threshold/relighting pass at the actual competition venue, not just once at home.
- No LARC-family rulebook found in this search documents a full safety-inspection checklist (RF check, battery/voltage check, weigh-in, puncture check) in explicit terms; the only sibling category with any inspection language (Line-Follower) treats it purely as a dimension check + build freeze. Do not assume the IAR Day-1 inspection will be more thorough than that without confirming with the organizers.
- The ARL/Colombian source's warning about 'heavy gases pooling in low areas, hard to clear by ventilation' applies to dense gases, not helium — helium is lighter than air and will instead accumulate near ceilings; ceiling-level ventilation/exhaust is the physically-correct mitigation for an indoor helium fill, but this specific point is an inference from physics, not stated in any source found.
- Direct primary-source fetches for OSHA/NIOSH helium/asphyxiation guidance mostly failed (403/404) this session; the 19.5%-O2 and CGA-SB-2/SB-28 figures came from a Wikipedia summary (secondary source) and should be verified against primary OSHA/CGA text before being treated as authoritative in a safety plan.
- BlimpSwarm firmware only calibrates ESC PWM range (microseconds), never force-in-grams — 'motors are calibrated' in their troubleshooting FAQ means something different from what the team needs (a thrust-in-grams verification against the 100gf rule).

### Gotchas

- BlimpSwarm firmware explicitly expects a Seeed XIAO_ESP32S3 board identity in the Arduino IDE board selector, and its PCB is a specific power-management/ESC/I2C-breakout design — the professor's custom PCB must have its GPIO map cross-checked against BlimpSwarm's schematic (docs/figs/Blimp_ESP_board_standalone_IC_power.sch) before assuming any of BlimpSwarm's example sketches (Bicopter1raw, BaseTranseiver) will run unmodified.
- esp-drone is quadcopter-shaped code (Crazyflie-derived): stabilization/mixing assumes 4 motors + attitude control for lift, not a bicopter/blimp gondola with buoyant lift and thrust-vectoring servos. Reuse means keeping the ESP32 radio/telemetry/sensor-driver layers and rewriting the control-mixing layer — do not expect a quadcopter flight-controller port to 'just work' on a blimp.
- esp-drone requires ESP-IDF v5.0; the CircuitDigest project (which the professor also sent) was built against the older ESP-IDF 4.4.5 — these are not automatically interchangeable; pick one baseline and note the IDF version used for reproducibility.
- esp-drone has had only 'limited support since December 2022' per its own README — do not assume active upstream fixes will land before the competition; any bug must be fixed by the team itself.
- PCBWay factory closures (Sep 25, Oct 1-4, GMT+8) sit inside week 1-2 of the available schedule. If the hardware audit concludes a respin is required, ordering after Sept 24 risks the order queueing through the closure before fabrication even starts, on top of whatever shipping transit time applies (unverified this session).
- IAR-LARC's own schedule page vs. the LARC-LARS main UMNG page disagree on event start date (Nov 2 vs Nov 4) — booking travel before confirming with organizers risks arriving on the wrong day for team registration/safety inspection.
- The actual IAR_LARC2026.pdf rules document (referenced on the rules page) was not retrievable via WebFetch this session (its Google Drive href wasn't exposed in the fetched HTML) — vehicle weight/size limits, allowed lift gas, safety-inspection checklist, and scoring rules are still unknown and must be pulled by a human opening the page directly in a browser.
