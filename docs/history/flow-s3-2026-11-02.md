# Optical flow through the S3, first runs (2026-11-02 evening)

**Where:** a house, not the flight room: no tape marks, and the people asked for no beeps,
so `software/flow_test.py` gained a window (`--window`) that shows when it records and takes
marks. **Vehicle:** the gondola only, motors off, on a height-adjustable desk.
**Camera:** the forward module (the 58° lens) flashed with `optflow_cam` at `aa13a19` by a
teammate and left in its front mount, so it looks at the room, not the floor; the team mounted
the ESP32-CAM board horizontally, and the image is rolled against the room (below).
**S3:** `i2c_tools` at `aa13a19` (408d96e plus the flow reader), flashed the same evening.
**Ground:** `software/flow_test.py` over the blimp's Wi-Fi; captures in the gitignored
`captures/` of the PC that ran them. The design and the pass marks: the optical-flow report,
§12.

## The link

`cam` showed nothing at first: 0 bytes on GPIO6 in 70 s, the S3 already on the new firmware
(`cam --zero` answered). After the team's fix on the camera side (not recorded which) the rows
came at 13 a second, 477 of the first 480 measured, 21 of 25 blocks matched, one rejected line
(the camera's power-up). From then on, through six runs and one Wi-Fi drop, every run had
12.5 frames a second with none lost.

## The runs

The desk goes from 110 to 70 cm and back at a constant speed, about 15–16 s each way (about
25 mm/s). At 1.40 m and the 58° lens (f = 144 px across 160 px) 40 cm is 41.1 px; at 1.5 m,
38.4 px. "Down" and "up" are the desk's; a move is the summed image shift from the start to
the end of one stretch of motion, found in the data (the marks agree within about 0.7 s).

gondola or the camera between them: the motion's direction in the image changed each time and
the ToF, which sees what is under the gondola, read 0.04 m in run 1 and 0.18–0.20 m after.

## The calibration

From runs 4 and 5 (`flow_test.py --calibrate … --dist 1.4 --moved 0.4`, written to the
gitignored `captures/flow-cal.json`): each run went down and back up the same 40 cm, so the four
moves should sum to zero; what they summed to, over their time, is a constant offset of
**(−0.004, +0.178) px/s** along the image axes. With it taken out the four moves read **34.2,
33.7, 34.4 and 34.9 px**, within 2 % of each other, against 31.8–37.3 raw; 34.3 px for 400 mm
at 1.40 m is **f = 120 px**, 83 % of the lens's 144.

Applied to run 6, which it was not made from (`--cal captures/flow-cal.json`):

| | Raw, f = 144 | Calibrated |
|---|---|---|
| Down | 305 mm (76 %) | **394 mm (99 %)** |
| Up | 369 mm (92 %) | **413 mm (103 %)** |
| Back at the start | 103 mm | **27 mm** |

The calibration lives only in the ground analysis: the camera and the S3 send and record the
raw shifts.

## Reasoned

- **The offset is the drift seen at rest**, 0.13–0.15 px/s in runs 4–5 against 0.178 from the
  moves: about 1.7 mm/s at 1.40 m, inside the 1–4 mm/s image-fixed offset the October bench
  measured (report §8.15). It makes every move against it read short and every move with it
  read long, which is why "down" read 15 % less than "up" in runs 3–5.
- **The 83 %** may be partly the mount: the desk's vertical motion lands 57° off the image's x
  axis, a diagonal, and the October bench read 92 % on diagonal motion against 108–116 % along
  an axis (§8.15, its open question 4). Not tested here: it needs the same runs with the camera
  upright.
- **Runs 1–3 do not calibrate anything.** Run 1's "up" read the 58° lens's 38 px at 1.5 m, run
  2's moves read as if the scene were 0.93 m away, run 3 lost blocks as its view changed: a
  scene of several depths and a camera or gondola moved between runs. Once the target was one
  flat wall and nothing moved between runs, the runs agreed within 1–3 %.

## Not established

- Any direction of motion but this one (the desk's vertical); other surfaces and distances;
  the floor seen from above, which is the flight case.
- Whether the offset holds from day to day (October: 1–4 mm/s between runs).
- The tilt test (the false shift a swing makes, against the gyro) and the rows' delay.
- What the team changed to bring the first rows in.
