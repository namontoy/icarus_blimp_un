# Free flights, 2026-11-01 and 02: the square's calibration and the first balloon follow

**Vehicle:** the gondola on the inflated envelope, forward camera (`target_cam`) mounted on the
gondola in every run, powered from test 4 on. **Firmware:** `i2c_tools` at `408d96e` (core 0.4.0,
40 % duty ceiling and 10 % deadband from boot); nothing was flashed during these two days.
**Ground:** `pid-tuning` with the `square_test.py`, `hold_test.py` and `blimp_link.py` changes
committed after this session (`--trim`, `--follow`, the floor check, the Wi-Fi retries). Every
flight is `square_test.py` (or `hold_test.py`) on the bench hold, with the bench parameter set
of `software/bench_params.txt` passed with `--param` (a reboot restores `params.c`'s simulator
placeholders). Captures are in the gitignored `captures/` of the PC that ran them, named below
by time stamp. How to run the next tests: `docs/flight-tests.md`.

**The room:** 15 x 6 m. A, B, C, D are the corners of a 2 m square taped on the floor; A to B
runs along a tape line with strips 1, 2, 3 and 4 m from A; C and D are on the far-wall side,
the blimp's left when it faces B from A. Positions below are the team's, judged by eye against
the tape.

Measured:

- **The rear pair's thrust leans forward.** The climbs went up and forward at about 45°, and
  the team confirmed the rear motors lean forward against the gondola (not fixable in hardware
  now). A constant −1 gf on the front pair (`--trim -1`) cancels it: H3 drifted 0.1–0.2 m
  forward against H0's 4 m. The rear pair runs at full for about half the climb and near 0.8
  while hovering, so the trim cannot grow much.
- **The air barely slows the blimp.** Test 3's 2 gf for 4 s carried it past 4 m: every push
  needs a brake.
- **The room's air pushes it toward the far wall,** 1–2 m in about 40 s and not the same from
  run to run. The trim cannot cancel that.
- The landing (0.1 m/s to 0.3 m, then 3 cm/s, motors off at a ToF of 0.09 m) was "very good" in
  every run; it dips about 10 cm where it slows at 0.3 m.
- The Wi-Fi ran at −79 to −86 dBm with the S3's antenna under the metallized envelope, and long
  console replies (the `param` listing) arrived in pieces; the tools now retry.

| check | (no capture; `cam_watch.py`) | motors off, the balloon on the 3 m strip in front of a new plain background, centre, left, centre, right, centre, hidden, shown | detected in 4 of 30 readings in the first 10 s (26–37 px), then never |

T3, from the capture (follow from 12.8 s):

- **The follow works when the camera sees the balloon.** Ahead, it held within 2–3° (the
  camera's 3° offset). The step to the left at about 18 s: it turned at up to 5°/s and faced
  the balloon in about 5 s without swinging back. Lost at 30 s, it turned to the last bearing
  (+7°) and held it steadily for 37 s. Seen again at 68 s, it turned to it in about 3 s (3°
  overshoot). From 86 to 92 s it followed the balloon out to +35°, about 4° behind. Height
  0.95–1.02 m throughout, yaw at most 8°/s.
- **The camera lost the balloon from 30 to 68 s** while it was at B, then right, B and hidden
  (the team: "it didn't turn when the balloon went back to B"). The rows kept coming at
  13 a second with nothing detected. Before the loss, the detected area was about 140 px ahead
  and 40–70 px after the step, where a whole 34 cm balloon at 2 m is about 470 px (f ≈ 144 px
  for the 160 px wide, 58° image): the camera was seeing a third of it or less. Around 72–78 s
  the area reached 900–1200 px.
- The team saw it move forward and a little toward C during the hover.
- **The battery sagged twice as far as before.** The cell put in for T3 read 3.67 V at rest and
  2.85–2.92 V through the whole hover (2.79 V lowest), where the previous cells hovered at
  3.3–3.4 V (H2, H3, test 4, 4c) and dipped to 3.1–3.4 V at most; it read 3.61 V at rest
  afterwards. The detections did not follow the voltage (100 %, then 0 %, then 96 % of ticks at
  the same 2.9 V), so the sag did not cause the loss; the cell or its connector is suspect.

The camera check afterwards, with the blimp still: no detection at any position from 10 s on,
and no false detection either in the whole minute.

## Reasoned

- **The IMU cannot see the drift.** In test 4 (`121230`) the body-x accelerometer read −37 mg
  settling, −19 mg pushing, −56 mg braking: steps of about ±18 mg, where a 3 gf push should give
  about 9 mg; the gondola pitched about 1° at the same moments, and 1° of tilt alone reads
  17.5 mg. In test 4c, on the floor before take-off, the reading wobbled ±1 mg (±0.15 mg over
  1 s averages); hovering, ±10 mg forward and ±26 mg sideways raw, ±1.8 and ±3.5 mg over 1 s
  averages, the rest being the blimp's slow swing; and the mean moved from −24 to −37 mg
  between the floor and the air. The air's drift (2 m in 40 s) is about 0.25 mg. Filtering the
  motors' vibration does not help: what remains is in the same band as the drift.
- **The brake is weaker than the push.** 3 s of brake did not stop 3 s of push, 4 s did, so
  the reverse push is about three quarters as effective even after `front_reverse_ratio`
  (0.74) scales the command for the motor's own reverse; probably the reversed wash blowing on
  the envelope.
- **The camera's detection, not the follow, failed in T3.** Partial detections at 2 m, then
  none at 3 m with a new background, with no false target: the balloon's red falling outside
  the detector's colours in this light, against this background. namontoy's `2423029` (pulled
  the same day, not flashed) finds the same red balloon moving 15–25° in hue between sessions
  with the white balance (`docs/learned-detector-report.md` §10.9–10.10).
- The rear motors cannot steer sideways in any useful way: they run at 0.8–1.0 to hold height,
  `core/` drives them equally, and the drift is a sensing problem first. The nose and the front
  pair can (`hold follow`).
