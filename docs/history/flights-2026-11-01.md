# Free flights, 2026-11-01 and 02: the square's calibration, the square, and the first balloon flights

**Vehicle:** the gondola on the inflated envelope, forward camera (`target_cam`) mounted on the
gondola in every run, powered from test 4 on. **Firmware:** `i2c_tools` at `408d96e` (core 0.4.0,
40 % duty ceiling and 10 % deadband from boot); nothing was flashed during these two days.
**Ground:** `pid-tuning` with the `square_test.py`, `hold_test.py` and `blimp_link.py` changes
committed after this session (`--trim`, `--follow`, the floor check, the Wi-Fi retries; then
`square_test.py --flight-bias-tau` and `target_test.py`, `fb50561`). Every
flight is `square_test.py`, `hold_test.py` or `target_test.py` on the bench hold, with the bench parameter set
of `software/bench_params.txt` passed with `--param` (a reboot restores `params.c`'s simulator
placeholders). Captures are in the gitignored `captures/` of the PC that ran them, named below
by time stamp. How to run the next tests: `docs/flight-tests.md`.

**The room:** 15 x 6 m. A, B, C, D are the corners of a 2 m square taped on the floor; A to B
runs along a tape line with strips 1, 2, 3 and 4 m from A; C and D are on the far-wall side,
the blimp's left when it faces B from A. Positions below are the team's, judged by eye against
the tape.

## A different battery type and test 5 (2026-11-02, afternoon)

From test 5 on, the cell was of another type, charged: 3.94 V at rest, 3.5–3.6 V hovering. The
whole blimp with it weighed **5.1 gf** heavy on the scale (4.7 gf on 2026-10-31). Each run is
one leg from A toward B, or a hover, all with the same push, coast and brake.

| Run | Capture | What was flown | What happened |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5 | `square-20261102-150052` | push 3 gf 3 s, coast 3.5 s, brake 3 gf 4 s, hold 10 s; trim −1 | went up, toward B, then backwards; landed about 2 m behind A |
| H5 | `square-20261102-150539` | climb, 20 s at 1 m, land; trim −1 | steady at first, then backwards: about 3 m behind A |
| H6 | `square-20261102-152129` | the same with trim −0.8 | steady for the first half, then backwards: about 1.5 m behind A |
| 5b | `square-20261102-152634` | test 5 with trim −0.6 | **reached 2 m and stopped there**; later, in the hold and the landing, it went back and away from D: landed about 0.5 m from A and 0.7 m to the side away from D |

All four flew cleanly (heading within 2.2°, height 0.94–1.09 m, the brake's dip 4 cm). The
rear pair hovered at 0.70–0.74 of its command, where the earlier cells needed 0.81–0.86.

Reasoned:

- **This battery makes the motors push harder for the same command.** The blimp is not lighter
  (5.1 gf), yet the rear pair needed about 14 % less command; the trim, a fixed command to the
  front pair, pushed more than 1 gf backwards. The front pair runs the trim at low power (13–16
  % duty), where a stronger cell adds proportionally more. From H5 and H6 the drift halves for
  each 0.2 gf less trim, so **−0.6 gf is the trim for this battery type**, against −1 gf for the
  earlier cells.
- Test 5's backward landing came mostly from the trim: H5's rate over test 5's 46 s is about
  4 m backwards, which leaves the leg itself near B, as 5b then showed.
- **The leg is right:** push 3 gf 3 s, coast 3.5 s, brake 3 gf 4 s stops it at 2 m. What
  follows (5b's 1.5 m back and 0.7 m sideways in the 23 s of hold and landing) is the open loop:
  a trim estimated from two rough distances on a draining cell, possibly a slow creep left by
  the brake, and the room's air, which on this afternoon pushed away from D (on 2026-11-01
  toward the far wall).

## The square, test 7 (2026-11-02, afternoon)

| Run | Capture | What was flown | What happened |
|---|---|---|---|
| 7 | `square-20261102-153447` | A → B → C → D → A at 1 m: each leg as 5b (push 3 gf 3 s, coast 3.5 s, brake 3 gf 4 s), 5 s still, a 90° left turn on the spot (8 s), 5 s still; trim −0.6; the new battery type, used (3.78 V at rest), by the team's choice | the legs' lengths "mostly fine" (team); after the turn at C it headed toward A instead of D, and was off from there |

Measured, from the capture: in `core/`'s own estimate the square was clean: the heading within
3.5° of its setpoint in every leg and hold, each turn reaching its 90° at up to 32 °/s, the
height 0.94–1.08 m. Replaying the estimator's bias filter offline over the recorded gyro
reproduces that estimate within about 1°; the gyro integrated with the bias held at its value
from the floor does not:

| After | `core/`'s heading | The replay of its filter | The gyro, bias fixed from the floor |
|---|---|---|---|
| turn 1 (at B) | 91° | 91° | 97° |
| turn 2 (at C) | 180° | 181° | **212°** |
| turn 3 (at D) | 270° | 271° | **340°** |
| leg 4 | 270° | 271° | **366°** |

Reasoned:

- **`core/`'s estimator kept learning the gyro's bias in the air** (`core/src/estimator.c`:
  whenever the gyro reads under about 11.5 °/s and the accelerometer near 1 g, tau 20 s,
  armed or not; `docs/10-known-issues.md`). A hover passes that test the whole time. Each left
  turn left about +0.5 °/s of real rotation in the bias, +1.7 °/s by the end, and the heading
  hold turned the blimp left at that rate while it believed it held its heading: about 212°
  after C, which is toward A (225°), not D (180°), as the team saw. The distances were right,
  so not the battery.
- **Workaround, no flash:** `square_test.py --flight-bias-tau 600` (and `target_test.py`, whose
  default it is) sets `gyro_bias_tau_s` 600 s once the estimator has converged on the floor and
  20 s again after the landing: 30 times slower learning, so the square's 96° would be about
  3°. Flown in all the balloon flights below; not yet in a square. **The fix is a `core/`
  change:** no bias learning while armed.

- **The ToF reads the floor to about 2.5 m in this room** and loses it beyond (flight 1), which

- Every flight from 2 on ended in the controlled landing, from 2 m.

Reasoned:

- **The nose lags a seen balloon by 11–20°** (flights 5 and 6) where T3, with the earlier cells

  where for a moment it gives nothing (the deadband, and the soft start waits for the motor to

- **The guard's 350 px** comes from flight 3 with the 34 cm balloon; a bigger balloon trips it

## Reasoned

- **The IMU cannot see the drift.** In test 4 (`121230`) the body-x accelerometer read −37 mg
  settling, −19 mg pushing, −56 mg braking: steps of about ±18 mg, where a 3 gf push should give
  about 9 mg; the gondola pitched about 1° at the same moments, and 1° of tilt alone reads
  17.5 mg. In test 4c, on the floor before take-off, the reading wobbled ±1 mg (±0.15 mg over
  1 s averages); hovering, ±10 mg forward and ±26 mg sideways raw, ±1.8 and ±3.5 mg over 1 s
  averages, the rest being the blimp's slow swing; and the mean moved from −24 to −37 mg
  between the floor and the air. The air's drift (2 m in 40 s) is about 0.25 mg. Filtering the
  motors' vibration does not help: what remains is in the same band as the drift.
- **The brake is weaker than the push.** 3 s of brake did not stop 3 s of push, 4 s did, so
  the reverse push is about three quarters as effective even after `front_reverse_ratio`
  (0.74) scales the command for the motor's own reverse; probably the reversed wash blowing on
  the envelope.
- **The camera's detection, not the follow, failed in T3.** Partial detections at 2 m, then
  none at 3 m with a new background, with no false target: the balloon's red falling outside
  the detector's colours in this light, against this background. namontoy's `2423029` (pulled
  the same day, not flashed) finds the same red balloon moving 15–25° in hue between sessions
  with the white balance (`docs/learned-detector-report.md` §10.9–10.10).
- **The trim weakens as the battery sags.** The rear pair is in the height loop: at 3.22 V it
  needed 6 % more command than at 3.40 V for the same lift (0.86 against 0.81, the ratio of the
  voltages). The trim is a fixed command to the front pair, so it then pushes about 6 % less,
  about 0.06 gf; with the 0.04 gf H3 left over (H0's 4 m and H3's 0.15 m in 37 s give about
  0.0056 m/s² per gf), about 0.1 gf drives 2.5–3 m in 100 s with nothing to slow it. The room's
  air makes up the rest of T3b's 4–5 m. The push and the brake are fixed commands too.
- **The follow's turns do not cause the forward drift:** in H3, 4c and both T3 flights the front
  pair's mean commanded push was the same −1 gf, and the corrections (|tz| 50–315 µN·m) were too
  small to change how much of it ran in reverse.
- The rear motors cannot steer sideways in any useful way: they run at 0.8–1.0 to hold height,
  `core/` drives them equally, and the drift is a sensing problem first. The nose and the front
  pair can (`hold follow`).
